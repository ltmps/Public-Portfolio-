SCExample / System Composer

Connect requirements to the parts that fulfill them.

The educational Mars-helicopter example separates operations, functions and physical subsystems. Architecture and allocation views make it possible to follow a requirement through the system and inspect the interfaces between components.

Work on the inherited SCExample teaching project began with System Composer models, stereotypes and allocation diagrams. Later development added typed interfaces, requirement traceability and executable mission-state behavior using synthetic events and educational assumptions.

Reaction wheels / Simscape & PID

Follow wheel torque into body motion.

The reaction-wheel study connects a multibody model with attitude-control scripts. Quaternion-based PID work, wheel sizing and log analysis explore how rotor inertia, torque and speed limits affect the commanded response.

Model checks exposed unit-conversion issues, inconsistent inertia assumptions and a mismatch between the tuning objective and the final controller. Those issues make parameter consistency and stress-case behavior central to the next model iteration. The scope here is simulated reaction-wheel dynamics.

These selected recordings illustrate simulated behavior under their stated assumptions. The broader study also includes stress cases that exceeded their tracking or wheel-speed limits.

Lambert launch-window comparison

This separate study uses solar two-body, prograde, zero-revolution Lambert transfers with Earth and Encke states from NASA JPL Horizons. Three selected cases emphasize departure energy, the sum of departure excess speed and arrival relative speed, or shorter flight time.

The contour map locates each selected departure date and flight time; the adjacent replay follows its propagated spacecraft path in physical time. The speed sum is a screening score. Numbers shown are outputs retained from the supplied study.

SHERPA / NASA RASC-AL concept

Turn a servicing mission into a system concept.

The student team's SHERPA proposal explored a multi-use platform near Earth–Moon L1 for spacecraft servicing, refueling and communications relay. Its concept of operations connects arrival, docking and servicing with architecture choices, technology assumptions, mass estimates and budget planning.

As part of the proposal team, I contributed to this shared mission-concept study.

A platform for refueling, repair and relay

The team presentation follows the proposed spacecraft from launch packaging to servicing operations near Earth–Moon L1. The CAD views connect the servicing deck, solar arrays and launch configuration.

ROS2 / ORBES integration

Exercise the data path before connecting every sensor.

ROS2 simulation work used CSV and IMU replay alongside synthetic camera data to exercise integration. Replayed and generated inputs provide a controlled setting for following data through the pipeline and separating interface behavior from physical sensor behavior.

The related ORBES case study follows the fan platform's hanging tests, frame-alignment investigation and motor-allocation work.
