Singularity

Bring the systems together.
Check how they separate.

Singularity was an UMBRA student rocket project at Cal Poly Pomona for the FAR 51025 competition. I was responsible for system integration and parachute packing, and performed GPS range tests. I also troubleshot integration issues and recovery pop tests, refining the ejection-charge setup.

Avionics & Recovery Engineer, Systems Liaison

Requirements

Select components for the recovery system.

The systems subteam defined requirements for altimeters, a GPS beacon and the recovery system. The team analyzed main-parachute descent rate and developed an avionics bay around the electronics and recovery interfaces.

Integration & packing

Fit the electronics and recovery connections together.

The team’s avionics bay accommodated GPS, redundant altimeters, batteries and wire access, with a printed sled and bulkhead interfaces carrying the assembly and recovery connections.

The launch checklist connected parachute preparation with vehicle integration: line condition, coordinated main-parachute folding, pack fit, harness connections across the payload and avionics bay, and clearance so cords could not catch on the payload.

View the recovery layout and parachutes

Altimeter testing

Troubleshoot the chamber, then repeat the test.

Initial vacuum-chamber tests encountered calibration problems. Troubleshooting and repeat tests checked the altimeters, alongside recovery pop tests and integration checks.

Test flight

Recovered intact, with an unexpected deployment.

The team reported roughly 3,900 ft on the test flight. The main parachute deployed prematurely, and the complete vehicle was recovered. Follow-up work included pop testing and investigation of retention at the main-parachute and avionics-bay connection.

GPS testing

From tracker comparison to range tests.

The initial GPS comparison favored Featherweight; the team later selected Big Red Bee as the trade study evolved.

I carried out a GPS field test across approximately six miles, with a stable connection between the two sites.

Fabrication

Make tools for fin installation.

Hands-on fabrication included tool and part assembly, laser cutting and bulkhead surface finishing. The fin-installation work included manufacturing custom hand tools and milling jig slots for alignment.
