The system

From sensor readings to motor commands.

The fan platform connects guidance commands and sensor estimates to attitude and translation control. A requested motion must pass two practical checks: do the sensor frames agree, and can the fourteen thrusters produce the requested force and torque?

Read the diagram as text

Guidance supplies setpoints from a predefined trajectory or tracking input. Navigation uses accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer and computer-vision inputs to provide attitude and translation estimates. These feed the attitude and translation controller, which sends thruster commands to the fourteen-motor vehicle.

Test stories

Follow the mismatch to a smaller test.

Hanging tests exposed disagreement in the state estimate. Later, allocator diagnostics traced missing motor output to requests the thrusters could not deliver.

Yaw aligned. Roll still disagreed.

Hanging tests exposed a camera/IMU frame mismatch and divergence in the translation estimate. Correcting yaw left a roll mismatch, so the investigation narrowed to a two-state Kalman test for yaw and body-rate bias.

A follow-up run showed the transient spike was gone. The smaller test isolated that behavior before testing returned to all three axes.

A command can ask for too much.

Allocator diagnostics found requested forces and torques outside the fourteen-thruster platform's feasible wrench envelope—the combinations of force and torque its thrusters could produce. Fallback commands could be negative; flooring those values to zero could leave no motor output.

Command logs and boundary plots traced the failure path and informed scaling the requested force and torque toward feasible commands.

From PID to LQR

Initial hardware-in-the-loop work used PID. The fan-platform control design then moved to a linear quadratic regulator (LQR) to coordinate coupled states. Sensor-fusion work addressed a separate question: what motion could be inferred from the available measurements?

Lessons from testing

Check the estimate, then the command.

The two investigations exposed different ways a control loop can fail before the intended motion reaches the robot.

State estimation

Check every axis after a frame correction.

The remaining roll mismatch after the yaw correction kept the investigation open. Narrowing the test to yaw and body-rate bias made a transient easier to isolate before returning to full-axis tests.

Actuation

Check what the motors can produce.

A requested force and torque can lie outside the thruster envelope. Command logs and boundary plots helped explain why flooring fallback values could produce zero output, and informed scaling toward feasible requests.
