The system

From sensor readings to motor commands.

I developed the overall guidance, navigation and control architecture for the ORBES fan platform, connecting guidance commands and sensor estimates to attitude and translation control.

Read the diagram as text

Guidance supplies setpoints from a predefined trajectory or tracking input. Navigation uses accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer and computer-vision inputs to provide attitude and translation estimates. These feed the attitude and translation controller, which sends thruster commands to the fourteen-motor vehicle.

Implementation details

The PID-to-LQR change is described below. Detailed implementation limits and supporting test records are not included yet.

From PID to LQR

I started with PID for initial hardware-in-the-loop work, then moved the fan-platform control design to a linear quadratic regulator (LQR) to coordinate coupled states. Separately, observability considerations informed the sensor-fusion work: what could be inferred from the available measurements shaped the state-estimation approach.

What this change tells us

This describes why I changed the control design. It does not establish a formal controllability or observability proof, a measured gain, or hardware validation of LQR.

Lessons & next steps

Control and estimation solve different problems.

The controller coordinates motion; the estimator works out that motion from sensor readings.

What I learned

Separate control from estimation.

The design work raised two distinct questions: how to coordinate coupled states, and what the sensor measurements could reveal about those states. The PID-to-LQR decision addressed the first; observability considerations informed sensor fusion for the second. The effect of that design change has not been documented here with test data.
