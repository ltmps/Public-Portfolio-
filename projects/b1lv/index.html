Hardware preparation

Connect the drawings to the parts on hand.

Organize the piping before assembly.

As a Propellant Feed Systems engineer, I helped organize piping and prepare hardware for cleaning and assembly. The team reviewed 20 launch-vehicle assembly P&ID drawings, cross-checked the Hardware Utilization List, identified missing parts and organized hardware into 15 P&ID segments.

Prepare a workable cleaning area.

When access to MOCAT was unavailable, the team prepared a controlled cleaning area in the Liquid Rocket Lab. A shared planning study compared an isolated room within the lab, designated whole-lab cleaning sessions and an outsourced cleanroom. The team favored designated lab sessions for cost, cleanliness control and practicality.

Compare pressurant layouts before committing to one.

The pressurant redesign study compared two conceptual layouts for safety, potential leakage reduction, compactness, ergonomics and simplicity. The study recorded qualitative tradeoffs to guide a later design choice.

Keep changes traceable.

I maintained meeting notes and design-change records and managed inventory through the Hardware Utilization List. Shared reporting covered piping, tank fatigue and water-flow testing, alongside co-authorship of the piping failure modes and effects analysis (FMEA). Contributions to the shared data-acquisition and control-system report covered mechanical relay operation and operating limitations.
