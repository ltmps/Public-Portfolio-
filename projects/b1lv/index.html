System & setup

Detail C · See the path before the data

The vehicle

Bronco 1 is the Liquid Rocket Lab launch vehicle: 17 ft long with a 7.8 in airframe, it feeds liquid oxygen and ethanol under pressure to the Ablative Chamber Engine, rated at 600 lbf initial thrust for a 15 s burn, toward an estimated 23,000 ft apogee. Its design was finished in 2019 to 2020, and the 2022 to 2023 team returned it to integration after the engine hot-fire campaign.

The test stand

The Mobile Rocket Engine Test Stand (MRETS) is the lab’s trailer-mounted stand for ground tests. It carried the 2022 hot-fire and integrated water flow tests of the engine, and in spring 2023 it hosted the B1LV water flow test with the lab’s DAQ box and LabVIEW control software.

Why it matters for B1LV

The oxidizer lines must be characterized before they carry liquid oxygen. Running water through a replica of those lines on MRETS gives measured pressure drops that set the static fire tank pressures and check the vehicle model, whose textbook loss coefficients are only estimates. A new strongback erector will hold the vehicle in launch configuration for later water and cold flow tests.

The replica isolates the oxidizer piping from the rest of the vehicle. Valves, fittings and instrumentation tie each measurement to its physical path.

Water-flow testing

Detail D · Trace discrepancies to the setup

Objective

Verify the flow characteristics of the Bronco 1 oxidizer feed system before it carries liquid oxygen, and use the measured flow to set the operating parameters for the first static fire.

Method

On 17 March 2023 the team ran distilled water through replica B1LV oxidizer piping on the Mobile Rocket Engine Test Stand. The DAQ recorded pressure, temperature and mass flow, and the team reduced each run, with its uncertainty, in its IWFT MATLAB application.

Outcome

The measured flow tracked the prediction closely, and the averaged block 1 values became the oxidizer-side inputs for the static fire. The scatter between paired runs was most likely caused by leakage at the extended piping, which is the issue traced below.

Issue 1 · Leak

A visible leak forced a reset of run 3. I tightened the leaking joints and re-ran it, but comparisons with earlier measurements stayed uncertain.

Issue 2 · Mass accounting

I analyzed the runs in the IWFT MATLAB application and found a collection-container error that needed a calculation correction.

I co-authored the post-test report on the leak investigation, retest and correction.

Reading Fig. 5

Fig. 5 plots tank pressure against oxidizer mass flow. Each blue point is a measured run with its error bar, and the purple points are the values the team predicted before the test.

The team expected pressure drop and mass flow to rise together as tank pressure rose, and both power-law fits follow that trend. The experimental fit is Ptank = 152.6 ṁ1.813, and the predicted fit is Ptank = 157.0 ṁ1.634.

The measured curve sits slightly below the predicted one across the tested range, so the piping passed a little more water at a given tank pressure than the model expected. The design-optimization model for the vehicle also draws on water-flow data, taking its injector efficiency spread from the 2022 integrated water flow test.

Tank model

Detail E · Simulation vs. hand calcs

The tanks

B1LV carries three tanks. A composite overwrapped pressure vessel (COPV) at the forward end stores nitrogen at 6,000 psig, and two pressure-reducing regulators feed that gas into the liquid oxygen and ethanol tanks in the aluminum mid-body. The propellant tanks are rated to a 330 psig maximum working pressure and are filled to leave at least 10% ullage, so boiling LOX has room before pressure climbs.

Why the pressurant system changed

The vehicle model ran the tanks at 300 psig set pressures and 90% fill. With the original single COPV, its pressure fell to 3,000 psig about 4 s into the planned 15 s burn, so the regulators could no longer hold tank pressure long before the propellant ran out. That result drove the redesign to a second COPV.

My part

I led the pressurant redesign concept review, which compared a shared manifold with one manifold per COPV: the first is lighter and simpler, and the second gives more control during fill. I also co-authored the design-optimization report, whose regulator sweep placed the provisional set pressures near 300 psia.

The tank-analysis report compared SolidWorks fatigue analysis with hand calculations.

Its conclusions depend on the loading assumptions, so the assumptions are stated with the result.

Lessons

Make assumptions visible

General notes

  1. Understand the measurement path.
  2. Examine uncertainty before using a nominal prediction.
  3. Record the change while the context is fresh.

Water flow test lessons

  1. Rehearse the whole procedure. The DAQ hardware and software were checked piece by piece, but a remote-access problem found on test day cost an hour. A dry run of the full procedure without sensors would have caught it.
  2. Secure electronics for transport. The myRIO controllers could slide off their mounts when the DAQ box was moved to the test site, so the team mounted them on a laser-cut board under a fitted cover.
  3. Choose parts that tolerate vibration. The mechanical relays that switch the solenoid valves can close under vibration and open a valve without a command. The team selected a solid-state MOSFET driver board to replace them.
  4. Size control wiring for its length. The cable from the remote control box to the on-site box was long enough that its resistance lowered the voltage reaching the ignitor circuit.
  5. Operate valves slowly. The FMEA added manual controls such as opening regulators and ball valves slowly, so pressure spikes do not corrupt the pressure readings, and inspecting every component before assembly.
